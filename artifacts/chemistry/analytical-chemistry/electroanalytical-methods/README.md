## How to reason through the material

Write a balanced equation or particle-level model first. Track atoms, charge, units, and conditions; show the calculation; then check whether the magnitude and direction agree with the chemical model.

For each item, make a three-column note: what it describes, what evidence or operation identifies it, and how changing one relevant condition changes the prediction. Then draw arrows between the items to show whether they represent prerequisites, steps in a process, alternative models, or ways of measuring the same system. Do not assume that two adjacent terms have a causal relationship unless the model or evidence supports it.

## Worked study example

Choose **electroanalytical methods (potentiometry, voltammetry)** from the list. State a concrete question that can be answered using it; write down the system boundary, observations available, assumptions, and expected result. Work from the definition or governing model to the result, and annotate each step with why it is valid. Change one condition and predict the effect before looking up the answer. If the prediction fails, identify whether the cause is a violated assumption, a measurement issue, or a mistaken link between concepts.
