# post-translational modification: mechanisms and methods

## Core idea

Build on **Structure of DNA and RNA: foundations**: retrieve its key model before adding this unit's ideas.

This lesson narrows **Molecular Biology** to a connected set of concepts. The scope terms below come from the subject outline; use them as a map of the model, not as a vocabulary list to memorize in isolation.

DNA is copied into RNA and used to direct protein synthesis, with regulation at multiple stages. Mutations can affect sequence or expression, but consequences depend on context. For this unit, focus on **post-translational modification** and how it fits the wider model.

## How to reason through the material

State the biological scale and hypothesis, identify the control and measured outcome, trace the proposed mechanism, and distinguish correlation from causal evidence. Consider biological variability and ethical limits.

For each item, make a three-column note: what it describes, what evidence or operation identifies it, and how changing one relevant condition changes the prediction. Then draw arrows between the items to show whether they represent prerequisites, steps in a process, alternative models, or ways of measuring the same system. Do not assume that two adjacent terms have a causal relationship unless the model or evidence supports it.

## Worked study example

Choose **post-translational modification** from the list. State a concrete question that can be answered using it; write down the system boundary, observations available, assumptions, and expected result. Work from the definition or governing model to the result, and annotate each step with why it is valid. Change one condition and predict the effect before looking up the answer. If the prediction fails, identify whether the cause is a violated assumption, a measurement issue, or a mistaken link between concepts.
