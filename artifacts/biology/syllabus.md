# Biology syllabus

This flexible four-stage sequence organizes the supplied outline. It is broad rather than jurisdiction-specific. A stage may be a term, semester, or self-study block. Teach prerequisite fluency alongside the main course; do not equate familiarity with terminology with ability to apply it.

## Outcomes

Learners should define important terms, explain central models and mechanisms, solve representative problems, check assumptions, interpret results, and communicate limitations. Projects should document the question, method, evidence, uncertainty, and conclusion.

## Readiness check

Begin with a low-stakes review of prerequisite vocabulary, quantitative or computational reasoning, graph/table interpretation, and safe working practices. Use results to plan support and parallel review.

## Lesson cycle

Retrieve earlier learning; define the system and vocabulary; teach one worked example with assumptions visible; guide a near-transfer problem; assign independent explanation and application; finish with an exit check. Revisit important ideas after about 1, 3, 7, and 21 days. Maintain an error log with corrected reasoning and a new example.

## Assessment

Use a readiness diagnostic, weekly formative checks, unit assessments combining explanation and application, and a cumulative project or portfolio. A strong response states assumptions, shows a traceable method, checks units or invariants where relevant, and interprets the result. Give feedback and a parallel reassessment opportunity.

## Project brief

Choose an investigation combining at least two topics. Require a question, assumptions, method, evidence/calculations, result, limitations, and a short peer-facing explanation. Laboratory, infrastructure, and security work must follow applicable safety rules and authorization boundaries.
