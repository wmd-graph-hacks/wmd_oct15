# Domains, PIDs, UFDs, and Polynomial Rings

An integral domain is a commutative ring with identity and no nonzero zero divisors. A principal ideal domain (PID) is a domain where every ideal is generated by one element. A unique factorization domain (UFD) has factorization into irreducibles unique up to units and order. Every PID is a UFD, but not every UFD is a PID.

For a field F, polynomial ring F[x] has a division algorithm: f=qg+r with r=0 or degree r<degree g. This makes F[x] a Euclidean domain, hence a PID and UFD. The Euclidean algorithm finds polynomial gcds and Bézout identities.

**Example.** In ℚ[x], divide x²−1 by x−1 to get quotient x+1 and remainder 0, so x−1 divides x²−1. Factorization x²−1=(x−1)(x+1) illustrates how roots give linear factors over a field. Over ℝ, x²+1 is irreducible; over ℂ it factors as (x−i)(x+i). Factorization depends on the coefficient field.

Irreducible and prime elements coincide in a UFD, but this requires hypotheses. When factoring, specify the ring: “irreducible over ℚ” differs from “irreducible over ℂ.”

## Lesson: factorization structures and polynomial rings

An integral domain is a commutative ring with identity and no zero divisors; cancellation holds for nonzero factors. A principal ideal domain (PID) has every ideal generated by one element. A unique factorization domain (UFD) has factorization into irreducibles unique up to order and units. Every PID is a UFD, but not every UFD is a PID. The integers and polynomial ring F[x] over a field are PIDs; multivariable polynomial rings are generally not PIDs.

In F[x], polynomial division with remainder supports the Euclidean algorithm. Irreducible polynomials play the role of primes. Over ℝ, x²+1 is irreducible; over ℂ it factors as (x−i)(x+i). Factorization depends on the coefficient field.

**Worked example.** In ℚ[x], divide x³−1 by x−1 to obtain x²+x+1 with remainder 0. Thus x³−1=(x−1)(x²+x+1). The quadratic has no rational root, so it is irreducible over ℚ, though its roots are complex.

**Practice with answers.** (1) ℤ is a PID and UFD. (2) A field is an integral domain. (3) x²−1 factors as (x−1)(x+1) over any field where the factors differ. (4) State the coefficient domain whenever claiming a polynomial is irreducible.

**Common pitfalls:** using “prime” and “irreducible” interchangeably without domain assumptions; forgetting units in uniqueness; and assuming a factorization is independent of the field.
